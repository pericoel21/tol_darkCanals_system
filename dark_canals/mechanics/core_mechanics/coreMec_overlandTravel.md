## Giving Indications
- GM doesn't tell the Players they are hexcrawling.
- Players don’t see the real map.
- Maps might be drawn by an NPC:
	- Will only include things that are relevant to that particular journey.
	- Directions will not be based on distance, but rather time spent travelling and landmarks on the way.
- From any area, the next league over can be seen well enough to see what kind of terrain it is.
- Larger settlements and tall landmarks can also be identified one league over, as well as from anywhere within a league.

## Procedures
### Random Encounters
Random encounters are rolled for each league of travel, and each watch spent not moving, but not all results are used de pending on wether you’re journeying or exploring.

| Journeying                        | Exploration                                    |
| --------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------- |
| Less granular                     | More granular                                  |
| Ignore inocuous random encounters | All random encounters happen unless not moving |

### Journeying
Journeying happens when the PCs know where they are going.
All trips back the way you came are journeying.

All PCs need to know is:
- Direction.
- How long the trip is likely to be.
- How much of each day they’ll be on the move.

Ignore all random encounters except the ones that pose some sort of threat or hazard. 

### Exploration
Exploration happens when PCs don't know where they're going.
- Travel is more granular.
- For watches spent not moving, ignore all non-threatening results.

> Each league across the map has something interesting in it, or moving through it, so each league travelled, each watch spent not moving, and each time anyone searches an area, roll for random encounters.

#### Looking for Something
When a PC wants to find something in a hex of wilderness:
- It requires an [[skill_observation|OBSERVATION]] [[coreMec_rollResolution|Roll]].
- It takes one [[coreMec_watches|Watch]].
- THe [[coreMec_difficulty|Difficulty]] is set by how hard the thing is to find.

If you succeed and the thing you wer looking for was in the hex, you find it.

If the thing you were looking for wasn’t in the area, or if you fail, you have a random encounter instead.

## The Night Watch
During the night you can:
- Sleep to recover [[stat_stamina|Stamina]] and recover from [[cond_exhaustion|Exhaustion]]
- Keep moving, if you're in a horry.

If PCs forgo camping or sleeping, they can cover extra distance, at the same rate as Travel Distance.

At sunrise if a character has not had at least one watch of sleep, they suffer [[cond_exhaustion|Exhaustion]].

## Dogwatches
The night watch is split into two dogwatches of three hours each.

Someone who spends one dogwatch asleep and one dogwatch as a lookout still avoids [[cond_exhaustion|Exhaustion]], but only recover as much [[stat_stamina|Stamina]] as hours at the normal rate.

So at the beginning of the night, if you want to have a lookout all night, pick one person to take each dogwatch. If you ever need to determine when during a watch something happens (like during which dogwatch the horrible monster appears), just roll a D6, the result is how many hours into the watch it occurs.

## Travel Distance
Leagues covered per [[coreMec_watches|Watch]]

| Terrain                 | Leagues |
| ----------------------- | ------- |
| Roads                   | 3       |
| Open terrain            | 3       |
| Difficult terrain       | 2       |
| Under inclement weather | 2       |
| In the dark             | 2       |
| Severe terrain          | 1       |
| [[horse\|Horse]]        | +1      |

> Examples of bad terrain
> - *Difficult terrain:* Sand dunes, forests, jungles, bogs, low mountains...
> - *Severe terrain:* High mountains

### Using a horse
- An unencumbered [[horse|Horse]] can cover an additional league per [[coreMec_watches|Watch]].
- Only pack [[horse|horses]] get this benefit over difficult terrain.
- No [[horse|horses]] get the benefit in the dark or over severe terrain.


Making Camp
It takes one person one watch to make camp. Any other characters can spend this watch
hunting, foraging, searching, repairing, crafting, cooking, eating, training, resting (recov-
ering Stress) or whatever else they feel like. Having made a camp makes any rolls for Ex-
posure EASIER.
Mechanic:
Nightfall & Daybreak
Each day, at the start of the evening watch, and at the start of the morning watch, the GM
will indicate that it is Nightfall or Daybreak respectively.
• At Nightfall you suffer one level of Exhaustion (pg62).
• At Daybreak you suffer one level of Starvation, Dehydration, and (if you’ve been in ex-
treme conditions), Exposure (see Conditions pg62).
• If your weapon was drawn/used in the last day, then at Daybreak your weapon becomes
Rusty if you don’t oil it (pg111).
THE FLOW OF TRAVEL
Exploration is only fun when you are discovering stuff, and getting from one place to an-
other can feel like a chore if it’s just a litany of annoying roadblocks to frustrate the players.
Be sensitive to the difference between Journeying and Exploration, and if it’s journeying
you’re doing, you only need to stop travel if you roll a hostile encounter, or if the players
choose to stop to do something. Players only need to roll if something hostile is sneaking
up on them, or they have to overcome a hazard, or for Exposure in hostile environments,
otherwise, just let the time pass, or give a brief sense of the landscape they pass. If it’s ex-
ploration, then every place they go needs something interesting, even if it’s just some
placid native fauna. Make sure to mark the passage of time with things like sunrise, noon,
sunset and so on, and let the players handle all their food and water and other resources.
