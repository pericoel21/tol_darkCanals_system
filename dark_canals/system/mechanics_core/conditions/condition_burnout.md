## Effect
Burnout reduces a Character's [[attribute_empathy|EMPATHY]] by one per level.

## Gaining Burnout
You suffer Burnout if a [[stat_stress|Stress Response]] roll (that otherwise would have succeeded) to fail.

## Recovering from Burnout
To recover a level of Burnout, a Character must expend one point of [[xp|XP]].

> Continually earning a single point of [[stat_resolve|Resolve]], then losing it, is a good way to lose [[attribute_empathy|EMPATHY]] real quick. Since Resolve is harder to regain than Stamina, either be careful about spending it, or make sure you’ve got the [[xp|XP]] to deal with the Burnout.

# Old (Deprecated)
Previously, you suffer Burnout on any day you spend your last Resolve.
This means that a) you can earn/spam Resolve on that day without consequence, b) you have to remember if you’ve suffered it already that day, and c) you are motivated to keep 1 Resolve because you reliably prevent Burnout by doing so. This is all lame.

Currently: This a) replaces the ‘just role-play Panic’, giving more meaningful consequence to it, b) means you can spend your last Resolve and continue playing (but now with a risk/reward decision, meaning every roll is more tense, kind of like with magic mishaps), and c) you don’t have to remember what you’ve done that day.

## Effect
Burnout reduces a Character's [[attribute_empathy|EMPATHY]] by one per level.

## Gaining Burnout
Once per day, when a PC hits zero [[stat_resolve|Resolve]], they gain a level of the Burnout [[mechanic_conditions|Condition]].

## Recovering from Burnout
To recover a level of Burnout, a Character must expend one point of [[xp|XP]].

> Continually earning a single point of [[stat_resolve|Resolve]], then losing it, is a good way to lose [[attribute_empathy|EMPATHY]] real quick. Since Resolve is harder to regain than Stamina, either be careful about spending it, or make sure you’ve got the [[xp|XP]] to deal with the Burnout.
