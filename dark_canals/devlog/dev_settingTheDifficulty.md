Setting the Difficulty
M
ANY RPGs are designed with ‘balance’ in mind - that the PC’s capabilities are evenly
matched to the challenges they will face. As a result, it is often part of the GM’s role
to punish players’ good ideas because those ideas might unbalance the game. The
world of Dark Canals is not balanced. Whenever a character does something in such a way
that would make things significantly easier for them, then they should be ‘rewarded’ for it by
it being EASIER for them. Incredible.
CIRCUMSTANCE BONUS
If a player wants a mechanical benefit from
the rules for something they are doing, they
get it by describing what they are doing to
improve their chances within the fiction. The
Difficulty mechanic is designed to reflect fic-
tional verisimilitude. If the players know
this, perhaps because they were the 1% of
players to bother reading the rulebook, they
will know that creative, lateral, in-character
thinking is what gets rewarded, not with
some sort of gameplay reward, but with sen-
sible consequences. Difficulty is a tool to make
the world make sense. Use it.
WHAT COUNTS AS SIGNIFICANT
Difficulty should be affected by anything that
isn’t how good the character is at the thing
they’re doing (their Skill and Proficiency).
How they do it, where they do it, when they
do it, with what tools they do it, under what
circumstances they do it - all these things can
affect Difficulty, so long as it makes a signifi-
cant difference.
Put mathematically, significant means at
least 10-15% difference in the probability.
Why? Because that is the minimum differ-
ence that humans can detect in changes in
probability. If it’s less that the minimum dif-
ference that humans can detect, just ignore
it.
USING ITEMS
Player characters can be Proficient at certain
things that use some sort of tool to accom-
plish, like fishing with a fishing rod, and this
is reflected by the +1 Modifier to their dice
pool that the GM doesn’t have anything to
do with. This doesn’t mean they’ve got a +1
fishing rod, or get a +1 for using the rod for
any old thing, the Proficiency is for the use of
the Skill, not an item benefit. As a general
rule then, items should not affect Difficulty,
they should just allow a character to do the
thing the item is used for - it’s assumed that
using an item for it’s intended purpose is
AVERAGE Difficulty. Sometimes, however,
characters might attempt something we
would normally associate with needing a
tool to accomplish, in this case it might be
HARD, VERY HARD or IMPOSSIBLE to accom-
plish without the item. Catching a fish with-
out fishing gear isn’t impossible, but it is
harder.
SPECIFIC SKILLS
Archery is hard. Have you tried it? Because
BALLISTICS is a pretty specialised field of skill
already, we don’t have to make all Skill rolls
for it HARD, as a baseline. TECHNE and REA-
SON on the other hand are very broad fields,
and have a huge number of niche applica-
tions that not everyone with a high rating in
them will automatically be good at. These
two Skills in particular should be treated as if
the Difficulty scale is absolute - if it is hard
for the average person, it should be HARD for
the PC, whereas even though archery is hard
for the average person, when a PC does it, it
should be considered AVERAGE as a baseline
because it’s already a niche application of
skill (see Specialist Skills pg21).
BASELINE
Apart from that, just remember that you are
rolling for a Skill only because there is a
chance of failure. If there is no chance (or less
than a significant chance) rolling may not be
necessary, so even AVERAGE is only useful
under circumstances that are putting pres-
sure on the character in some way. That be-

PROBABILITIES
The effect Difficulty has on the probability of
success is not linear, so Difficulty makes a big
difference (as does The Modifier, at least in
the middle of the probability curve) so these
should be used very deliberately. It does, how-
ever solve the day-to-day expert problem - if
a doctor (with a WITS Attribute of 4 and a
SURGERY Skill of 3) performing a routine
surgery only rolls seven dice (73.66% chance
of success) are you really going to let them
put you under the knife? The GM could eas-
ily set the Difficulty for this task to VERY
EASY (93.75% chance) because it is routine
for that character, which would feel much
better. This way you can roll on routine
things if you want, rather than just hand-
waving them. Or if success is assured, the roll
might just be to see how well you do it.
ME WANT MATH
Oh, you want all the numbers, do you?
Think it’ll help you play the game? Look,
they’re here, ok, but don’t look at them. Just
say that at AVERAGE Difficulty, it’s about
~10% for every die you’re rolling (skewed at
the top and bottom, sure) and about +/-15%
for going up or down a Difficulty, ok? OK?

THE DANCE OF THE MODIFIERS
Because of the way the character sheet is
handled, and because Difficulty and
Modifiers are handled in fundamentally
different ways, you'll never suffer what I
call the 'dance of the modifiers,' where you
have to hold a bunch of positive and neg-
ative numbers in your head after you
roll. It also means that you never need
the GM’s permission to build your dice
pool, as the GM can never affect how
many dice you roll.
CRUNCH
There are some specific mechanics for com-
bat, and exploration and so on, but more-or-
less, all the crunchiness of the game boils down
to whether there is one or more significant
factors to change the Difficulty of the roll.
Do you really need a table of wind speed
thresholds to tell you whether it’s harder to
shoot someone with an arrow? No! If it’s sig-
nificantly windy, then shooting is HARD, if
it’s anything less than significantly windy,
then it’s not! Then you call it, roll it and
move right on ahead.
