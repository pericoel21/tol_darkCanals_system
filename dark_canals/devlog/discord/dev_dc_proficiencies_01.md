    # My proposal
    
    ## Proficiencies as tags
    
    Three ways to use Proficiencies:
    
    - _As Keys:_ To be able to perform an action in the first place.
    - _As Mods:_ They give you the modifier. This should be the default for Proficiencies because the modifier, as explained in the current rules, is mainly obtained due to proficiencies.
    - _As circumstantial Help:_ Lowering the difficulty of a task. This is a possible use of Proficiencies, but I wouldn't do it that much (or at all) because Difficulty is defined in the rules as "factors external to the character".
    
    Two ways to re-design the Proficiency system:
    
    - _Freeform:_ Allow Proficiencies to be invoked as Keys, Mods or even Help arbitrarily at the GM's discretion.
    - _Hard-Coded:_ Each Proficiency is used in a specific way (either allowing for rolls in Key ones or modifying them in Mod ones).

    ## Why this proposal fits
    
    There's already prose in the current rules talking about Specialized Skills vs Skills with areas of Expertise. Essentially, somebody with Ballistics knows how to shoot a bow, but a high Techne character might not necessarily know how to pick locks. Well, this is backed by my proposal: You might have a good Ballistics stats, but having a Proficiency in artillery gives you one more die when shooting a cannon that you wouldn't get with, say, a javelin. Therefore, in this universe, bowmen, hunters, dwarf engineers and baseball players all are very accurate: they have good Ballistics, but they are defined by Proficiencies. At the same time, having a good Teche shouldn't make you a decent cheff, thief, clock maker, wood carver, bowyer, guitar player... Those all require a proficiency to even attempt the skill! The greatest luthier in the realm will have Techne through the roof... And they might still be unable to play piano because those are different Proficiencies.
    
4. ## What does this mean for character creation
    
    I see two paths:
    
    - PC creation stays pretty similar, but the definitions of all Proficiencies might fit in a spread, in a table (if you want to also define if they are Keys or Mods); or even be unnecessary (if Proficiencies are tag words that get adjudicated on the spot).
    - PC creation becomes a bit more freeform, where the GM asks the player: When you spent time as a sailor, which proficiency did you learn? The GM has to give the green light, but otherwise this system is pretty easy.

	Yep, I’m on board. I’ve been thinking about this just recently as well, and I think the rules have evolved to the point that the game lends itself to this kind of thinking viz. magic techniques/forms and weapon traits etc.

Here’s what I was thinking though - the **Mechanic: Specialist Skill** can be re-worked to enable the free-form tag system to avoid having to have different kinds of tags.

If all tags were mods - all of them were niche applications of a Skill, and all gave +1 to that niche application - but we allowed the GM to set the Difficulty of specialised Skill use to Hard, Very Hard, **Impossible without Proficiency**, or Impossible, then we’d prevent having to classify tags as mods or keys (but still allow key functionality), prevent the GM having to know what tags a PC has in order to set Difficulty, and the players wouldn’t have to decide between mod tags and key tags.

For example, there’s a Surgery proficiency currently called Organ Harvest that allows you to harvest a monster part as an artefact. Under a freeform tag system, a PC might have proficiency in carving up animals (e.g. for food), but if they attempted to harvest the heart of a wendigo (or whatever), the GM might rule that as Very Hard, but maybe they’d rule it Impossible without Proficiency in medical surgery (or equivalent), depending on the setting.
