NON-PLAYER CREATURES
There’s a lot of non-player characters out
there, and that’s why the GM requires a dif-
ferent set of tools to to play the game than
the players do. This chapter contains the
tools you need to create them (on the fly, if
necessary) and ‘run’ them - or what we like
to call role-play them.
RESOLUTION & SYMMETRY
For the players to interact with the world in
an intuitive way, the world needs to function
in essentially the same way as their charac-
ters do. Once they’ve learned the basics of
playing their characters, they shouldn’t also
have to learn a separate system to interact
with the non-player characters (including
horrible monsters). This means there needs
to be a kind of symmetry between the PCs and
how they work and the NPCs and how they
work.
The GM, however, has to role-play these
NPCs, and there are lots of them, so if the
PCs operate just under the cognitive load of
the players, then there’s a good chance play-
ing many NPCs will overload the GM if
they’re the same complexity as the PCs. The
solution is to have NPCs operate the same
way as PCs, just at a lower resolution. They’ll
have the same range of capabilities, just not
the granularity. This means that when a
player affects an NPC in some way, it’ll have
a bigger effect on them, for better or for
worse, and we’ve found these bigger more
obvious swings to be more significant and
perceptible to the players. As a GM, it just
means you’ll have less fidelity with your
characters, but less work to do, too.
REPOSITORY
The most interesting and natural way for
players to find information in the imaginary
world is by talking to NPCs. So make sure
they’re everywhere!
ROLLIN’
Ideally, the GM won’t be rolling anything
more than the occasional D6, and we’ll do
anything (short of compromising verisimili-
tude) to prevent the GM from having to
form and roll a dice pool for NPC actions,
but just in case, the NPCs will be capable of
this if you desperately need to.
