YOUR CHARACTER IS NOT YOU
They’re not your avatar, they’re not your
power fantasy self-insert ego trip. You are
not here to ask yourself ‘what would I do in
this situation?’ You are here to have an expe-
rience of what it would be like for you to be
like your character, not the other way
around.
If your ego isn’t tied up in your character,
you’re free to role-play them in whatever
way suits the character best, rather than do
whatever it is that makes you feel good for
having somehow ‘won’ the game, like argu-
ing over rules minutiae. In fact, to be a good
role-player you should be driving your PC
towards what makes the most sense for the
character, and sometimes, that’s failing at
stuff.
Imagine if in every film each actor wasn’t
trying to play their character, but to have
their character ‘win’ the story and never fail.
It would be boring! Just like a role-play ses-
sion full of Mary-Sue characters who always
act optimally, as if with the meta-knowledge
that they’re only characters in a game.
People are flawed. They’re complicated.
They’re irrational. They’re interesting.
They’re compelling. That’s the kind of char-
acter you want in a role-playing game. Drive
your character to their fate, be it tragic or tri-
umphant, and remember, your character ul-
timately failing, is not you failing as a player,
it might just be the best piece of role-play
you ever get to do.
YOU’RE BORING
There’s no sugar coating it. You are. But
that’s because you’re smart - you think things
through, anticipate problems, don’t take un-
necessary risks and you try to get on with
people. That’s all very commendable, but for
a character in some sort of dramatic and dan-
gerous imaginary world? Utterly banal.
People often desire to insert themselves into
fiction so that they can see their real-life
opinions and politics and beliefs get re-
warded, perhaps because of a lack of that sort
of affirmation and validation in their actual
lives. So they make atheists in worlds where
the gods literally come down and smite peo-
ple and they make social justice warriors in
worlds where unequivocally evil creatures
exist with the sole purpose of being evil, and
regardless of whether these things are cor-
rect in the real world they make for horrible,
non-sensical, boring characters in our RPGs.
Please, don’t bring you into the game world,
rather, bring the game world to life in your
mind.
