VIOLENT TENDENCIES
Violence is one of those things that seems a
given in some genres of fiction, and although
we (generally) develop some distaste for it in
real life, it holds a special place in fiction as
having some of the most dire consequences
for failure of any scenario. It gets special
treatment then, not because it forms the bulk
of the game you play, or because it’s encour-
aged in any way, but rather because the
minutiae might mean the difference between
life and death.
Oh, and I guess at some point maybe we do
relish a certain kind of anti-social release,
where we’re justified in enacting righteous
violence on those that absolutely deserve it. I
have no doubt that undead are so popular an
antagonist at least partly because they’re just
people you don’t have to feel bad about mur-
dering. Well, that and they’re a corruption of
the natural order, a fusion of the impossible,
mutually exclusive categories of dead and
alive. But I’m not really here to make com-
ment on that! What I’m trying to do is justify
why so much of the book is focussed on vio-
lence, when violence is no more the focus of
the game than any other facet of role-play-
ing. It’s important because it’s consequences
can be so final.
